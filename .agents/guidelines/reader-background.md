# Reader background

The requested accessibility target is an interested first-year undergraduate, roughly age 18, who can follow basic mathematical arguments. Age is an aspiration, not a reliable specification of knowledge. Preserve this ambition when defining a target's concrete prerequisite profile.

For the JMLR pilot, use this provisional profile until the writing specification settles any material differences: comfortable with algebra, functions, finite sets and sums, elementary inequalities, sequences/limits, basic probability and expectation, and direct or inductive proofs. Do not presume specialized reinforcement learning, stochastic approximation, martingale or stopping-time knowledge merely because a journal referee might have it.

The writing specification must identify advanced prerequisites that the manuscript will introduce, dependencies it will state and cite, and any knowledge the user agrees to assume. If the detailed probability proof requires more background than this profile, surface the specific gap during specification; do not silently replace the target with an expert audience or expand the paper into a textbook. A short prerequisite note or precisely stated supporting result may suffice.

JMLR's own baseline is an interested reader with a machine-learning background but no special knowledge of the subject. That is a venue criterion; the user's accessibility goal can be more demanding.

For each exposition session, give the checker the agreed prerequisite profile directly, not the author specification or status file. Record whether a difficulty is a prerequisite gap, a manuscript explanation gap, a notation problem, or a reader mistake. The writer should not add remedial material for a prerequisite explicitly included in the agreed profile without a reason.

A weaker model is an inexpensive comprehension probe. It can miss something a student would understand, or recall advanced material a student would not know. Record the actual model and assistance received. Treat success as evidence about that test, not as measured human comprehension or proof correctness.
