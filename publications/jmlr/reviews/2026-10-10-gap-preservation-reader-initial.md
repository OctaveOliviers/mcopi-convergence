## What is claimed

The main theorem (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 4--11) concerns initial-visit Monte Carlo optimistic policy iteration on a finite discounted or proper episodic MDP. At each iteration the algorithm chooses a deterministic policy greedy for its current estimate, samples one initial state-action pair, and moves that pair's estimate toward a noisy return under the chosen policy (`active/2-mcopi.tex`, lines 162--180 and 220--245). The sampling restriction is weaker than uniform sampling over all state-action pairs: after a state is chosen, its action is uniform, but different states may be selected at unequal and history-dependent rates (`active/2-mcopi.tex`, lines 256--271; `active/3-results.tex`, lines 28--45). Every pair must nevertheless receive infinite accumulated learning weight.

Under the standing assumptions, the theorem says that the policy action-value target (q_{\pi_k}) changes only finitely many times almost surely, the estimates (q_k) converge almost surely to the optimal action values (q_{\pi_*}), and every sufficiently late selected policy is optimal. The policy itself need not become constant when several optimal actions tie; eventual constancy is asserted only when every state has a unique optimal action.

## How I understand the proof

The proof separates the deterministic tendency from the stochastic obstruction.

1. **Uniform action updates make every mean-field policy change an improvement.** In the noise-averaged recursion, all actions at a fixed state have the same update probability. If the next greedy policy chooses a new action, comparing its updated estimate with the old action's updated estimate lets one cancel the common update factor. Since the old action was greedy before the update and (0\leq\alpha_k\mu_k(s)\leq1), the new action cannot have lower *true target value* under the current policy (`active/results/1-mean-field.tex`, lines 41--63). The policy-improvement theorem then gives (v_{\pi_{k+1}}\geq v_{\pi_k}). Chaining this inequality while the action-value target is fixed shows that every transition between distinct targets is strict (`active/results/1-mean-field.tex`, lines 65--67). Finitely many deterministic policies then imply that the mean-field target eventually stops changing; accumulated learning moves every estimate to that target, and a policy occurring infinitely often is greedy for its own limiting values, hence optimal (`active/z-appendix.tex`, lines 112--123).

2. **A stochastic transition is safe if no truly worse action becomes greedy.** For a reference policy (\pi=\pi_t), the set (\mathcal B_\pi) contains precisely the state-action pairs whose true (q_\pi)-value is below that of the reference action (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 20--26). For each such pair, the estimated gap (d_k(s,a)=q_k(s,\pi(s))-q_k(s,a)) is tracked until the next distinct target or until the estimates leave a fixed deterministic bound (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 28--47). Because action sampling is uniform within the state, its conditional drift pulls it toward the positive true gap (d_\pi(s,a)), and its conditional noise variance is proportional to the same per-action state probability (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 49--63). This matching factor is what permits adaptive and arbitrarily sparse state selection.

3. **Seeding creates a margin without requiring a lower bound on state-selection probability.** While a state is unfinished, the proof requests a favorable update: either raise an underestimated reference action or lower a competing action. A one-sided second-moment bound gives a fixed conditional chance of the needed return, and uniform action selection gives a fixed conditional chance (p_0>0) once that state is selected (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 81--99). Up to (N) such requests per bad pair give a gap at least the smaller of a fixed threshold and a constant multiple of (N\alpha_{T_s}), where each state has its own completion time (T_s) (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 101--122). Inertia is essential here: favorable updates keep the reference action greedy, and no update at a state retains its previous action.

4. **A change of measure forces the finite seed sequence while leaving completed states honest.** Directly conditioning on every future seed success could alter later returns at states whose seeds have already finished. The auxiliary law instead reweights only the requested favorable updates. Its density is a product of success indicators divided by their conditional success probabilities, with at most |\(\mathcal S\times\mathcal A\)|\(N\) nontrivial factors (`active/z-appendix.tex`, lines 134--163). The density process has conditional mean one; later factors average out when examining the next update. Consequently, state-selection probabilities remain unchanged and the action-return law at completed states is the original one (`active/z-appendix.tex`, lines 166--189). The bounded density also transfers an auxiliary-law success probability back to the original law at a cost of (p_0^{|\mathcal S\times\mathcal A|N}).

5. **The barrier estimate protects a completed gap at the learning-rate scale.** Before a gap reaches the fixed level (\delta), its target is at least (4\delta), so while the estimate lies in ((0,\delta)) it has positive drift at least (3\delta\alpha_k\sigma_k(s)). The proof measures time by the accumulated learning (A_s=\sum\alpha_k\sigma_k(s)), not by iteration count (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 154--168). A crossing of zero would require a negative martingale excursion large enough to defeat both the starting gap and the accumulated positive drift. Dividing possible crossing times into dyadic ranges of (A_s) and applying the supplied stopped maximal inequality produces a bound proportional to (\alpha_{t'}/d_{t'}) (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 169--199; `active/z-appendix.tex`, lines 192--202). After the gap first reaches (\delta), deterministic drift cannot push it below (\delta); a later crossing therefore requires a martingale movement of at least (\delta/2), controlled by the square-summable tail (\sum_{k\geq t'}\alpha_k^2) (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 201--216). This is why an (N\alpha_{T_s})-sized seed can suffice even though it may shrink to zero at late restarts.

6. **Each sufficiently late continuing interval has a fixed positive chance of progress.** Choose (N) large enough to make the sum of the first barrier terms small, then start late enough that the square-sum tail is small. Under the auxiliary law, all seeds succeed; a union bound over the finitely many bad pairs then makes all completed gaps survive through the next target transition with probability at least (1/2) (`active/results/3-convergence.tex`, lines 18--44). Unfinished states still use the reference action, and states completing at the endpoint have a positive gap there. Thus a finite transition before exit cannot select a truly worse action; the strict policy-improvement lemma makes it a strict improvement (`active/results/3-convergence.tex`, lines 46--56). Transfer back to the original law yields one uniform positive probability (p) of exit, an infinite constant-target interval, or strict improvement.

7. **Repeated chances force eventual stopping of target changes.** From every continuing interval, (m) consecutive successful intervals or earlier stopping has conditional probability at least (p^m); this uses repeated conditioning at finite stopping times, not independence (`active/results/3-convergence.tex`, lines 62--66). There cannot be |\(\mathcal P\)| consecutive strict improvements among only |\(\mathcal P\)| deterministic policies. Hence survival through successive blocks has probability at most ((1-p^{|\mathcal P|})^j\), which tends to zero (`active/results/3-convergence.tex`, lines 67--71). The previously proved almost-sure boundedness lets one choose, path by path after a countable intersection over deterministic restarts, a restart for which the artificial exit never occurs (`active/results/3-convergence.tex`, line 73). The target therefore changes only finitely often. Fixed-target averaging then forces convergence to an optimal target (`active/2-mcopi.tex`, lines 403--414), and finiteness plus a positive optimality gap excludes every nonoptimal action from late greedy selections (`active/results/3-convergence.tex`, line 75).

## Why the assumptions matter

- Finiteness gives finitely many policies, a positive global minimum (\delta) over all nonzero bad-action gaps, finite union bounds, and positive gaps separating nonoptimal actions at the end.
- Discounting or proper episodic termination makes values finite and supports the policy-improvement theorem and uniform return moments (`active/2-mcopi.tex`, lines 112--149 and 239--246).
- Uniform initial actions within a state give the common drift factor needed both in the mean-field cancellation and in the gap recursion. State frequencies need not be uniformly bounded below because the proof uses conditional success given state selection and the state-specific learning clock.
- Initial-visit updating makes the two possible updates in a gap disjoint, yielding the variance bound proportional to (\sigma_k(s)) (`active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 54--62).
- Inertial tie-breaking preserves an action at a state with no update and preserves the reference action throughout successful seeding.
- Infinite accumulated learning is used in fixed-target averaging, so a final constant target is actually learned (`active/z-appendix.tex`, lines 65--86).
- Square summability makes the late martingale-noise tail small; (\alpha_k\to0) makes late seed-scale estimates small; the weakened monotonicity comparison controls every later step size by the scale at the restart or seed completion.
- The conditional zero mean and second-moment bound supply both the martingale estimates and a fixed one-sided probability for favorable seed returns.
- Uniqueness of each state's optimal action is used only for eventual policy constancy, not for value convergence or eventual optimality.

This is a demanding passage for the stated reader, but it supplies the specialized conditional-expectation and change-of-measure reasoning rather than assuming it.

3. **Random and possibly very late state completion** — At `active/results/2-probability-to-improve.tex`, lines 101--115, I initially worried that a state could wait forever while other completed gaps accumulate noise.
