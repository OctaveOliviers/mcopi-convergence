## Initial account, retained from the reader

The main claim is that initial-visit MC-O-PI converges almost surely in any finite discounted or proper episodic MDP under substantially weaker sampling symmetry than earlier general results: actions must be sampled uniformly within each selected state, but states themselves may be sampled at unequal, adaptive rates. Each state must accumulate infinite learning weight. Returns must be conditionally unbiased with bounded variance, the learning rates must be square summable with bounded future increases, and greedy-policy ties use full inertia. The estimate converges to q_pi*, target action-value tables change only finitely often, and all sufficiently late selected policies are optimal. Unique optimal actions imply eventual policy-label constancy.

The proof treats q_{pi_k}, rather than the noisy estimate q_k, as the important evolving object.

1. Lemmas 1–2 establish the decision-process foundations. Properness gives a uniform geometric termination tail even for history-dependent control, ensuring finite values and permitting verification against arbitrary control laws. Policy improvement then converts a statewise comparison under q_pi into v_{pi'}>=v_pi and q_{pi'}>=q_pi.
2. Lemmas 4–6 handle stochastic-approximation housekeeping. Conditional infinite learning weight becomes actual infinite learning weight because the sampling discrepancy has a convergent square-summable martingale series. The filtering lemma shows that convergent accumulated noise cannot prevent a scalar recursion from following an eventual target. Consequently q_k is ultimately bounded by the finite collection of policy targets, and an eventually constant target forces convergence to the optimal table.
3. Proposition 9 exposes why action-wise uniformity is the useful symmetry. At one state, every action receives the same mean-field coefficient c. Greediness before and after the update can therefore be rearranged to show that the newly selected action is at least as good under the old policy’s true action values. Policy improvement makes the target sequence monotone. Since there are finitely many targets, the deterministic mean field eventually has one target and converges to the optimum.
4. Noise destroys one-step monotonicity, so the stochastic proof works on an interval where the target q_pi remains fixed and before q_k leaves a fixed ball. A “bad” action is one whose true q_pi-value is below that of the reference action pi(s). If every estimated bad-action gap stays positive, a future greedy policy cannot choose a genuinely worsening action.
5. Lemma 13 supplies a barrier for one such gap on that state’s accumulated-learning clock. Its drift pulls toward a true gap of at least 4delta, while its variance carries the same state-sampling factor. This permits arbitrarily long calendar-time pauses. Square summability controls the late noise, and bounded future step increases relate the gap created at completion to all relevant later steps.
6. Lemma 14 constructs positive “seed” gaps. Whenever an unfinished state is selected, a favorable return either raises the reference coordinate or lowers one bad coordinate. Full inertia keeps the reference action selected while this construction proceeds. After at most JN requested successes, each completed state has a quantified positive margin.
7. Because different states may finish at widely separated times, simply conditioning on all seed successes would distort later return laws at states already completed. Lemma 15 instead reweights only requested seed updates. Under this auxiliary law the seeds succeed, but state selection and completed-state updates keep their original conditional laws. The likelihood bound transfers any resulting progress probability back to the original algorithm.
8. Lemma 16 combines the seeds and barriers. With uniformly positive conditional probability, a fixed-target interval either leaves the stopped ball, persists forever, or ends at a strictly componentwise better target. The scalar rank W(pi)=sum_i q_pi(i) strictly increases in the third case.
9. There are finitely many possible W-values. A block of sufficiently many successful intervals must therefore be absorbed by exit or persistence; it cannot consist entirely of strict increases. Repeated conditional lower bounds make infinite target changes before exit probability zero. Stability then permits a deterministic restart after which exit never occurs, so only finitely many target changes occur globally. Lemma 6 yields q_k -> q_pi*.

The assumptions enter separately and visibly: finiteness gives finite policies/targets, a positive minimum bad gap and finite rank; discounting/properness gives well-behaved returns/verification; initial visits equate actual update and initial-pair probability; action uniformity gives common coefficients/drift; cumulative learning forgets initialization; square summability controls noise; bounded increases compare earlier seeds to completion/later noise; inertia preserves reference actions; unique actions give label constancy.

2. Why do return-dependent future requests leave a completed state's update law unchanged, and what information is that law conditioned on?

## Answers, retained

1. Each request factor has conditional mean one. Although request times may be unbounded, at most JN factors differ from one, so 0<=L_k<=p0^(-JN) and L_k eventually becomes constant on every path. Thus L_k->L_infinity almost surely under a deterministic integrable bound. The exact fact needed is conditional bounded convergence: E[L_infinity|F'_t]=lim_k E[L_k|F'_t]=L_t=1. No optional-stopping claim at an unbounded request time is needed. For random t, the text applies this fixed-time argument separately on each event {t=j}.

2. Let R_{k+1}=L_{k+1}/L_k be the current likelihood factor. Future request factors disappear after conditioning on the next history because E[L_infinity|F'_{k+1}]=L_{k+1}. Consequently, for bounded Y determined by F'_{k+1}, Ehat[Y|F'_k,S_{k+1}=s]=E[R_{k+1}Y|F'_k,S_{k+1}=s]. At a completed state there is no current request, hence R_{k+1}=1. Its next-update law is therefore unchanged. The law is conditioned on the current post-selection history F'_k and the selected state S_{k+1}=s; it includes the conditional initial-action and return law. Return-dependent requests at later times do not bias it because their likelihood factors have conditional mean one after the current next history is fixed.

3. At every alive target interval, Lemma 16 gives P(absorption or strict W increase|F'_t)>=p. Iterating with the tower property gives P(absorption within m intervals or m consecutive strict increases|F'_t)>=p^m. If L is the number of distinct possible W-values, L consecutive strict increases are impossible. Therefore, from every surviving block boundary, P(absorption within L intervals|current history)>=p^L. Hence survival of j blocks is at most (1-p^L)^j ->0. Unsuccessful transitions may lower W, but they only begin another block with the same uniform conditional chance of absorption. Independence is unnecessary because the lower bound holds conditional on every realized history and is composed using the tower property.
