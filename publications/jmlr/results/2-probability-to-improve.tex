\subsection{Initial-visit MC-O-PI converges with uniform updates}
This section proves that stochastic perturbations in initial-visit MC-O-PI cannot consistently offset the policy improvement of mean-field solutions. As a result, the algorithm converges to $\qopt$ when updates are uniform over the actions of each state.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm: convergence mcopi uniform}
Under \autoref{asp: standing}, the action-value targets $(q_{\pi_k})$ change only finitely many times almost surely, and
\[
q_k\longrightarrow q_{\pi_*}\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Every sufficiently late selected policy is optimal. If every state has a unique optimal action, the selected policy is eventually constant.
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection{Proof strategy}
\label{sec: limitations of existing methods}
The mean-field argument improves the policy at every transition. A sampled update can instead worsen it, so we seek a fixed positive probability of improvement at each sufficiently late transition. Since only finitely many deterministic policies exist, repeated opportunities for improvement will then imply convergence.

This follows the combined stability--ODE strategy of \citet[Section~5.4]{Kushner2003StochasticApplications}. The difficulty is that a trajectory can enter a policy region $Q(\pi)$ arbitrarily close to its boundary. Recurrence to that region alone does not give repeated visits to the fixed interior subset used in a standard lock-in estimate; see the setup in \citet[Section~3]{Borkar2002OnApproximation}.
We therefore first create a positive separation between the currently selected action and actions that would worsen the policy, and then control the probability that noise removes that separation. A sufficiently large fixed multiple of the current learning rate is enough: the separation may tend to zero at successive entries. Condition~\eqref{eq:comparison} makes the resulting probability bound uniform over sufficiently late entries. It is a sufficient condition for this proof, not a claimed necessity for convergence of the algorithm.

\subsubsection{Actions that would worsen the policy}
Consider a policy $\pi$ selected at a time $t$. The pairs
\[
\mathcal B_\pi=\big\{(s,a)\in\mathcal S\times\mathcal A:
q_\pi(s,a)<q_\pi(s,\pi(s))\big\}
\]
would worsen its selected action according to $q_\pi$. If none of these pairs becomes greedy, then at the next transition the new policy satisfies the Policy Improvement Theorem. To express this condition in terms of the estimates, we compare their estimated action values.

\begin{definition}[Gap variables]
\label{def: gap variables}
Fix a deterministic bound $K>\max_{\pi\in\mathcal P}\|q_\pi\|$ and a deterministic restart $r$. Let
\[
\zeta_r=\inf\{k\ge r:\|q_k\|>K\}
\]
be the first time the estimate leaves this bound.
For a stopping time $t\ge r$, on $\{t<\infty,t<\zeta_r\}$ set $\pi=\pi_t$ and let
\[
\tau=\inf\{k>t:q_{\pi_k}\ne q_\pi\}
\]
be the next change of the action-value target.
For each pair $(s,a)\in\mathcal B_\pi$ and each $k\ge t$, define
\begin{align}
\label{eq:gaps}
d_k(s,a)&=q_k(s,\pi(s))-q_k(s,a),\\
d_\pi(s,a)&=q_\pi(s,\pi(s))-q_\pi(s,a).
\end{align}
The gap noise is $\xi_k(s,a)=w_k(s,\pi(s))-w_k(s,a)$.
\end{definition}

The target remains $q_\pi$ before $\tau$, even if the selected policy changes. Equal update probabilities within each state therefore give the gap recursion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gapdrift}
d_{k+1}(s,a)=d_k(s,a)+\alpha_k\big(\sigma_k(s)(d_\pi(s,a)-d_k(s,a))+\xi_k(s,a)\big).
\end{equation}
Thus, on average, the estimate gap is pulled towards the positive target gap $d_\pi(s,a)$. The noise has the following bounds for $t\le k<\tau\wedge\zeta_r$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gapvariance}
\mathbb E[\xi_k(s,a)\mid\mathcal F'_k]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\xi_k(s,a)^2\mid\mathcal F'_k]\le c_\xi\sigma_k(s),
\qquad c_\xi=2(c_v+4K^2).
\end{equation}
Indeed, only the initial pair is updated. The two possible updates forming this gap are disjoint, each has probability $\sigma_k(s)$, and before $\zeta_r$ the difference between an estimate and its target has magnitude at most $2K$. Their total second moment bounds the variance in \eqref{eq:gapvariance}.
The stopping time $\zeta_r$ is used only to justify these deterministic bounds. Boundedness will remove it at the end of the proof; we never condition the noise on future containment.

We first determine how large a positive gap must be for stochastic updates to preserve it with high probability. We then construct that margin through favorable updates, a step called \emph{seeding}, and combine the two estimates to obtain a positive probability of policy improvement.

Choose
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:constants}
\delta=\frac14\min_{\substack{\pi\in\mathcal P\\(s,a)\in\mathcal B_\pi}}d_\pi(s,a)>0.
\end{equation}
If the set in this minimum is empty, the Policy Improvement Theorem applies between every two policies in both directions. All policies then have the same optimal values, and \autoref{thm: suboptimal blocks finite} proves the theorem directly. We henceforth consider the nonempty case.

\subsubsection{Preserving positive gaps}
The following estimate bounds the probability that a positive gap closes before the next target change or exit. The drift and noise variance in \eqref{eq:gapdrift}--\eqref{eq:gapvariance} both carry the per-action update probability $\sigma_k(s)$ at state $s$. Measuring accumulated learning at that state will let us control this probability even when its updates are arbitrarily far apart. Write
\[
\bar\alpha_k=\sup_{j\ge k}\alpha_j
\]
for the largest remaining learning rate.

\begin{lemma}[Gap preservation]
\label{lem:barrier}
Consider a pair $(s,a)\in\mathcal B_\pi$ in \autoref{def: gap variables}, and a stopping time $t'\ge t$ with $t'<\infty$, $t'\le\tau\wedge\zeta_r$ and $d_{t'}(s,a)>0$.
Assume deterministic learning rates $0<\alpha_k\le1$ with $\sum_k\alpha_k^2<\infty$. Suppose that, under a given probability law, the gap recursion \eqref{eq:gapdrift} and conditional moments \eqref{eq:gapvariance} hold for $t'\le k<\tau\wedge\zeta_r$. Under that law,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:barrier}
\mathbb P\!\left(\exists\text{ finite }n\in[t',\tau\wedge\zeta_r]:d_n(s,a)\le0\ \middle|\ \mathcal F'_{t'}\right)
\le \frac{4c_\xi}{3\delta}\frac{\bar\alpha_{t'}}{d_{t'}(s,a)}
+\frac{4c_\xi}{\delta^2}\sum_{k=t'}^\infty\alpha_k^2.
\end{equation}
The event in \eqref{eq:barrier} includes the update ending at $\tau\wedge\zeta_r$.
\end{lemma}
Equation~\eqref{eq:barrier} bounds the chance that one specified gap closes. If its starting value is bounded below by a fixed positive number, both terms become arbitrarily small as $t^{\prime}$ increases. If the starting gap also tends to zero, however, the first term depends on the ratio of the largest remaining learning rate to that gap: waiting alone need not make it small. A sufficiently large multiple of that learning rate controls this ratio, while a sufficiently late start makes the squared-learning-rate tail small. After proving the estimate, we construct such a margin through favorable updates.

\begin{proof}
Fix $(s,a)$ and write $d_k=d_k(s,a)$ within this proof. Stop the noise sum at the first time the gap $d_n$ reaches zero or below, or at the endpoint $\tau\wedge\zeta_r$, whichever comes first:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta&=(\tau\wedge\zeta_r)\wedge\inf\{n\ge t':d_n\le0\},\\
M_n&=\sum_{k=t'}^{n-1}\mathbf1_{\{k<\eta\}}\alpha_k\xi_k(s,a).
\end{aligned}
\]
Define the accumulated learning at state $s$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clock}
A_n=\sum_{k=t'}^{n-1}\alpha_k\sigma_k(s).
\end{equation}
We distinguish whether the gap $d_k$ reaches or exceeds $\delta$ before it first becomes nonpositive.

\emph{The gap $d_n$ reaches zero or below before reaching $\delta$.}

We consider the event that $d_n\le0$ at a finite time $n\in[t',\tau\wedge\zeta_r]$, with $0<d_k<\delta$ at every preceding time $t'\le k<n$. Thus reaching zero includes equality, and reaching the upper level $\delta$ means reaching or exceeding it. We first determine how negative the accumulated noise $M_n$ must be to bring the gap $d_n$ to zero or below, then bound the probability of that noise fluctuation.

For $k\ge t'$, recall that the stopped noise sum $M_n$ retains increment $k$ exactly when $k<\eta$, where $\eta$ is the first time the gap becomes nonpositive or the endpoint $\tau\wedge\zeta_r$ is reached. The indicator $\mathbf1_{\{k<\eta\}}$ and the learning rate $\alpha_k$ are known from $\mathcal F'_k$. Condition~\eqref{eq:gapvariance} applies when $k<\eta$; when $k\ge\eta$, the stopped increment is zero. Substituting the noise increment $\alpha_k\xi_k(s,a)$ and taking these known factors outside the conditional expectation, the zero-mean condition~\eqref{eq:gapvariance} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[M_{k+1}-M_k\mid\mathcal F'_k]
&=\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{k<\eta\}}\alpha_k\xi_k(s,a)
\mid\mathcal F'_k]\\
&=\mathbf1_{\{k<\eta\}}\alpha_k
\mathbb E[\xi_k(s,a)\mid\mathcal F'_k]\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-noise-mean}
\end{equation}

Because the conditional mean in \eqref{eq:gap-noise-mean} is zero, the conditional variance is the conditional second moment. Squaring an indicator leaves it unchanged. We again take the known factors outside the conditional expectation and apply the variance bound \eqref{eq:gapvariance}. For the last step, use $\alpha_k\le\bar\alpha_{t'}$ and the accumulated-learning identity $A_{k+1}-A_k=\alpha_k\sigma_k(s)$ from \eqref{eq:clock}. For $k\ge t'$, this gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[(M_{k+1}-M_k)^2\mid\mathcal F'_k]
&=\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{k<\eta\}}\alpha_k^2\xi_k(s,a)^2
\mid\mathcal F'_k]\\
&=\mathbf1_{\{k<\eta\}}\alpha_k^2
\mathbb E[\xi_k(s,a)^2\mid\mathcal F'_k]\\
&\le\mathbf1_{\{k<\eta\}}c_\xi\alpha_k^2\sigma_k(s)\\
&\le c_\xi\alpha_k^2\sigma_k(s)\\
&\le c_\xi\bar\alpha_{t'}(A_{k+1}-A_k).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-noise-second-moment}
\end{equation}

Suppose now that the gap first becomes nonpositive at $n$, without previously reaching $\delta$. For $t'\le k<n$, the gap satisfies $0<d_k<\delta$, whereas \eqref{eq:constants} gives the target gap $d_\pi(s,a)\ge4\delta$. The mean-field drift term $\alpha_k\sigma_k(s)(d_\pi(s,a)-d_k)$ in \eqref{eq:gapdrift} is therefore at least $3\delta\alpha_k\sigma_k(s)$. Summing \eqref{eq:gapdrift}, with every noise increment up to this first nonpositive gap retained in $M_n$, gives
\begin{equation}
0\ge d_n\ge d_{t'}+3\delta A_n+M_n.
\label{eq:gap-before-noise}
\end{equation}
Thus the accumulated noise $M_n$ must offset both the starting gap $d_{t'}$ and the accumulated mean-field increase $3\delta A_n$.

Write $b_n$ for their sum. To control the probability that the noise $M_n$ falls to $-b_n$ or below, rescale each noise increment by the corresponding threshold:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
b_n&=d_{t'}+3\delta A_n,\\
Z_n&=\sum_{k=t'}^{n-1}\frac{M_{k+1}-M_k}{b_{k+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-rescaling}
\end{equation}
We will relate a downward crossing of $-b_n$ by $M_n$ to a fixed level for the rescaled noise $|Z_n|$.

We consider $n>t'$, as is necessarily the case when the positive starting gap $d_{t'}$ first becomes nonpositive. Both noise sums start at zero: $M_{t'}=Z_{t'}=0$. By \eqref{eq:gap-rescaling}, $M_{k+1}-M_k=b_{k+1}(Z_{k+1}-Z_k)$. When we expand and sum these differences, each $Z_k$ with $t'<k<n$ appears twice: once multiplied by $b_k$, and once subtracted with multiplier $b_{k+1}$. The term involving $Z_{t'}$ vanishes, leaving the endpoint term $b_nZ_n$. This is summation by parts, and it gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
M_n
&=\sum_{k=t'}^{n-1}b_{k+1}(Z_{k+1}-Z_k)\\
&=\sum_{k=t'+1}^{n}b_kZ_k
-\sum_{k=t'}^{n-1}b_{k+1}Z_k\\
&=b_nZ_n-\sum_{k=t'+1}^{n-1}(b_{k+1}-b_k)Z_k.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-summation-parts}
\end{equation}

The thresholds $b_k$ are positive and nondecreasing because the accumulated learning $A_k$ is nondecreasing. Hence the weights $b_{k+1}-b_k$ are nonnegative and sum to $b_n-b_{t'+1}\le b_n$. Applying the triangle inequality to \eqref{eq:gap-summation-parts}, then bounding each $|Z_k|$ by its largest value up to $n$, yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
|M_n|
&\le b_n|Z_n|
+\sum_{k=t'+1}^{n-1}(b_{k+1}-b_k)|Z_k|\\
&\le
\left[b_n+\sum_{k=t'+1}^{n-1}(b_{k+1}-b_k)\right]
\max_{t'\le m\le n}|Z_m|\\
&\le2b_n\max_{t'\le m\le n}|Z_m|.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-noise-max}
\end{equation}

If the gap $d_n$ reaches zero or below before reaching $\delta$, \eqref{eq:gap-before-noise} requires $M_n\le-b_n$. Equation~\eqref{eq:gap-noise-max} then forces $|Z_m|\ge1/2$ at some finite time $m\in[t',n]$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb P\!\left(
\begin{array}{l}
\exists\text{ finite }n\in[t',\tau\wedge\zeta_r]:d_n\le0,\\
0<d_k<\delta\text{ for every }t'\le k<n
\end{array}
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_{t'}\right)\\
&\qquad\le
\mathbb P\!\left(\exists\text{ finite }m\ge t':|Z_m|\ge\tfrac12
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_{t'}\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-before-event}
\end{equation}

To bound the probability on the right of \eqref{eq:gap-before-event}, we use Doob's martingale maximal inequality. It bounds the probability that a martingale reaches a given magnitude by its expected squared terminal value divided by the square of that magnitude. We justify its application here before estimating the resulting second moments.

Doob's inequality requires square integrability. The denominator $b_{k+1}=b_k+3\delta\alpha_k\sigma_k(s)$ is known from $\mathcal F'_k$, so dividing the increment in \eqref{eq:gap-noise-mean} by $b_{k+1}$ preserves its conditional zero mean whenever the rescaled increment is integrable. However, the positive starting gap $d_{t'}$ may be arbitrarily small, so integrability needs checking.

For this check only, fix $\varepsilon>0$ and multiply the process $Z_n$ by $\mathbf1_{\{d_{t'}\ge\varepsilon\}}$. This leaves the noise sum $Z_n$ unchanged on histories with $d_{t'}\ge\varepsilon$, and sets it to zero on the remaining histories. The indicator is known at the restart. Since $b_{k+1}\ge d_{t'}$, equation~\eqref{eq:gap-noise-second-moment} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\!\left[
\left(\mathbf1_{\{d_{t'}\ge\varepsilon\}}(Z_{k+1}-Z_k)\right)^2
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_k\right]\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^2}
\mathbb E[(M_{k+1}-M_k)^2\mid\mathcal F'_k]\\
&\qquad\le\frac{c_\xi\alpha_k^2}{\varepsilon^2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-cutoff-moment}
\end{equation}
The modified increments have conditional mean zero, and \eqref{eq:gap-cutoff-moment}, together with $\sum_k\alpha_k^2<\infty$, makes the modified noise sum square-integrable.

First suppose the restart $t'$ is a fixed finite time, and take a finite horizon $n\ge t'$. Apply Doob's inequality at magnitude $1/2$ to this square-integrable noise sum. The conditional version follows by applying the ordinary inequality after multiplying the noise sum by the indicator of any event known at the restart. Also, distinct zero-mean increments have zero expected product: when the later increment is drawn, the earlier one is already known and the later one has conditional mean zero. Thus the expected squared terminal value equals the expected sum of squared increments. On histories with $d_{t'}\ge\varepsilon$, the modified noise sum equals $Z_n$, and we obtain
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb P\!\left(\max_{t'\le m\le n}|Z_m|\ge\tfrac12
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_{t'}\right)\\
&\qquad\le\frac{\mathbb E[Z_n^2\mid\mathcal F'_{t'}]}{(1/2)^2}\\
&\qquad=4\,\mathbb E\!\left[
\sum_{k=t'}^{n-1}(Z_{k+1}-Z_k)^2
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_{t'}\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-doob-finite}
\end{equation}

If the restart $t'$ is random, apply this argument separately on $\{t'=j\}$. This event is known at time $j$, so the same indicator argument applies there; combining the countably many finite values of $j$ gives \eqref{eq:gap-doob-finite} at the random restart.

We can now remove the temporary cutoff. For each $\varepsilon=1,1/2,1/3,\ldots$, \eqref{eq:gap-doob-finite} holds on $\{d_{t'}\ge\varepsilon\}$. These events cover every positive starting gap $d_{t'}$, so \eqref{eq:gap-doob-finite} holds for every such gap, outside one null event. The finite second-moment bound \eqref{eq:gap-cutoff-moment} need not be uniform as $\varepsilon$ decreases: the inequality \eqref{eq:gap-doob-finite} that it justifies contains no $\varepsilon$. No subsequent calculation is restricted to a fixed positive lower bound on $d_{t'}$.

Finally increase the finite horizon $n$. The events on the left of \eqref{eq:gap-doob-finite} increase to the event that $|Z_m|$ reaches $1/2$ at some finite time, while the nonnegative sums on the right increase to the full sum. Monotone convergence therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb P\!\left(\exists\text{ finite }m\ge t':|Z_m|\ge\tfrac12
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_{t'}\right)\\
&\qquad\le
4\,\mathbb E\!\left[
\sum_{k\ge t'}(Z_{k+1}-Z_k)^2
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_{t'}\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-doob-infinite}
\end{equation}
Together, \eqref{eq:gap-before-event} and \eqref{eq:gap-doob-infinite} explain why bounding the squared increments of the rescaled noise $Z_n$ bounds the probability that the gap $d_n$ reaches zero or below before reaching $\delta$.

The denominator $b_{k+1}$ is known from $\mathcal F'_k$, so it can be taken outside the conditional expectation. Substituting \eqref{eq:gap-noise-second-moment} into the definition \eqref{eq:gap-rescaling} yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[(Z_{k+1}-Z_k)^2\mid\mathcal F'_k]
&=\frac{\mathbb E[(M_{k+1}-M_k)^2\mid\mathcal F'_k]}{b_{k+1}^2}\\
&\le
\frac{c_\xi\bar\alpha_{t'}(A_{k+1}-A_k)}
{(d_{t'}+3\delta A_{k+1})^2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-rescaled-moment}
\end{equation}
The tower property lets us average these conditional second-moment bounds at the starting history $\mathcal F'_{t'}$ and sum them in \eqref{eq:gap-doob-infinite}.

To estimate the resulting sum, the function $x\mapsto(d_{t'}+3\delta x)^{-2}$ is decreasing. A rectangle with width $A_{k+1}-A_k$ and height at the right endpoint $A_{k+1}$ lies below its graph. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\frac{A_{k+1}-A_k}{(d_{t'}+3\delta A_{k+1})^2}
\le
\int_{A_k}^{A_{k+1}}\frac{\mathrm dx}{(d_{t'}+3\delta x)^2}.
\label{eq:gap-integral-comparison}
\end{equation}
The intervals $[A_k,A_{k+1}]$ are consecutive and start at $A_{t'}=0$. Extending them to $[0,\infty)$ gives an upper bound even if the accumulated learning $A_n$ has a finite limit. The integral depends only on the starting gap $d_{t'}$, so its conditional expectation given $\mathcal F'_{t'}$ is unchanged.

Combining \eqref{eq:gap-before-event}, \eqref{eq:gap-doob-infinite}, \eqref{eq:gap-rescaled-moment} and \eqref{eq:gap-integral-comparison}, the conditional probability that the gap $d_n$ reaches zero or below before reaching $\delta$, by time $\tau\wedge\zeta_r$, is at most
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&4\,\mathbb E\!\left[
\sum_{k\ge t'}
\frac{c_\xi\bar\alpha_{t'}(A_{k+1}-A_k)}
{(d_{t'}+3\delta A_{k+1})^2}
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_{t'}\right]\\
&\quad\le
4c_\xi\bar\alpha_{t'}\int_0^\infty
\frac{\mathrm dx}{(d_{t'}+3\delta x)^2}\\
&\quad=\frac{4c_\xi\bar\alpha_{t'}}{3\delta d_{t'}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-before-bound}
\end{equation}
This bound applies even when the positive starting gaps $d_{t'}$ tend to zero at successive entries. Its size depends on the ratio $\bar\alpha_{t'}/d_{t'}$; seeding will ensure that the starting gaps are sufficiently large relative to the remaining learning rates. It remains to bound the probability that the gap $d_n$ reaches zero or below after first reaching $\delta$.

\emph{The gap $d_n$ reaches zero or below after reaching $\delta$.}
Suppose that the first nonpositive gap occurs at a finite time $n\le\tau\wedge\zeta_r$, and that $d_k\ge\delta$ at some preceding time $t'\le k<n$. Let $\ell$ be the last such time. We first show that falling from $d_\ell\ge\delta$ to $d_n\le0$ requires a noise fluctuation of at least $\delta$.

In the update at $\ell$, the noise-free part is a convex combination of the gap $d_\ell\ge\delta$ and the target gap $d_\pi(s,a)\ge4\delta$, since $0\le\alpha_\ell\sigma_\ell(s)\le1$. It is therefore at least $\delta$. Equation~\eqref{eq:gapdrift} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
d_{\ell+1}
&=(1-\alpha_\ell\sigma_\ell(s))d_\ell
+\alpha_\ell\sigma_\ell(s)d_\pi(s,a)
+(M_{\ell+1}-M_\ell)\\
&\ge\delta+M_{\ell+1}-M_\ell.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-after-first-update}
\end{equation}
At every subsequent time $\ell<k<n$, the gap satisfies $0<d_k<\delta$ by the choices of $n$ and $\ell$. The mean-field drift in \eqref{eq:gapdrift} is then nonnegative. Summing the remaining updates and using \eqref{eq:gap-after-first-update}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
0\ge d_n\ge\delta+M_n-M_\ell.
\label{eq:gap-after-noise}
\end{equation}
Thus $M_n-M_\ell\le-\delta$. By the triangle inequality,
\begin{equation}
\delta\le|M_n-M_\ell|
\le|M_n|+|M_\ell|
\le2\max_{t'\le m\le n}|M_m|.
\label{eq:gap-after-max}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the stopped noise sum $M_m$ must reach magnitude $\delta/2$ at some finite time. The last-visit index $\ell$ is used only in these pathwise inequalities; conditioning remains at the stopping time $t'$.

By \eqref{eq:gap-noise-second-moment} and $\sigma_k(s)\le1$, the conditional second moments of the increments of $M_m$ are at most $c_\xi\alpha_k^2$. Their sum is finite, so $M_m$ is square-integrable without a cutoff on the starting gap $d_{t'}$. Applying Doob's inequality at magnitude $\delta/2$, with the conditional and infinite-horizon steps justified above, bounds the probability of this second case by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb P\!\left(\exists\text{ finite }m\ge t':|M_m|\ge\tfrac\delta2
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_{t'}\right)\\
&\qquad\le\frac{4}{\delta^2}\,
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{k\ge t'}(M_{k+1}-M_k)^2
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F'_{t'}\right]\\
&\qquad\le\frac{4c_\xi}{\delta^2}\sum_{k\ge t'}\alpha_k^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gap-after-bound}
\end{equation}
Both cases retain the noise increment ending at $\tau\wedge\zeta_r$. Adding the bounds \eqref{eq:gap-before-bound} and \eqref{eq:gap-after-bound} proves \eqref{eq:barrier}.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Seed: creating positive gaps}
The preservation bound \eqref{eq:barrier} now gives us a concrete objective: lower-bound its denominator $d_{t'}(s,a)$ relative to the largest remaining learning rate. At a newly selected policy, some estimated gaps may be zero. We create the required margin through a finite number of favorable updates. States may wait arbitrarily long between updates, so each state completes this construction separately; its gaps will then be protected while other states are still waiting.

Suppose the reference action $\pi(s)$ is still selected and $d_k(s,a)<\delta$. Since
\[
\big(q_\pi(s,\pi(s))-q_k(s,\pi(s))\big)
+\big(q_k(s,a)-q_\pi(s,a)\big)
=d_\pi(s,a)-d_k(s,a)>3\delta,
\]
at least one of these two estimation errors exceeds $3\delta/2$. We request a favorable update as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $q_\pi(s,\pi(s))-q_k(s,\pi(s))>3\delta/2$, update $(s,\pi(s))$ and require $v_k(s,\pi(s))\ge-\delta/2$.
\item Otherwise update $(s,a)$ and require $v_k(s,a)\le\delta/2$; in this case $q_k(s,a)-q_\pi(s,a)>3\delta/2$.
\end{enumerate}
Either update increases the intended gap by more than $\delta\alpha_k$, leaves every other gap against the reference action unchanged or larger, and keeps the reference action greedy. The selection rule \eqref{eq:greedy-pol-tie-breaking} therefore retains it.

The conditional return moments ensure that each one-sided noise requirement has probability at least $\delta^2/(4c_v+\delta^2)$, by the one-sided Chebyshev inequality (see Appendix~\ref{app: one sided return bound} for the conditional calculation). Conditional on selecting the state, each action is sampled uniformly. Thus every favorable update has conditional probability at least
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:localprob}
p_0=\frac{\delta^2}{\max_{s\in\mathcal S}|\mathcal A(s)|\,(4c_v+\delta^2)}>0.
\end{equation}
No lower bound on the probability of selecting the state is needed.

\begin{definition}[Seed construction]
\label{def: seed construction}
For the gaps and stopping times in \autoref{def: gap variables}, fix a positive integer $N$. To give every gap an initial margin, order the actions in $\mathcal B_\pi$ at each state. Skip a pair if its gap has already reached $\delta$; otherwise request up to $N$ favorable updates for it, stopping earlier if it reaches $\delta$. Whenever an unfinished state is sampled, request the next favorable update on its list. Completed states receive ordinary updates. Stop the construction at the first failed request or at $\tau\wedge\zeta_r$.
Write $T_s$ for the iteration when state $s$ completes its list, with $T_s=t$ if it is complete initially and $T_s=\infty$ if the construction stops first. Skips are processed at $t$ and after every successful update, before the next sample, so $T_s$ is a stopping time.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Seed]
\label{lem:seeds}
Under \autoref{asp: standing}, consider the gaps and stopping times of \autoref{def: gap variables}, with $t\ge k_\alpha$, and let $N$ be a positive integer. The seed construction of \autoref{def: seed construction} requests at most $|\mathcal S\times\mathcal A|N$ favorable updates. Along successful requests, each unfinished state retains its reference action. If $T_s<\infty$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:seedmargin}
d_{T_s}(s,a)\ge\min\{\delta,N\delta\bar\alpha_{T_s}/c_\alpha\}
\qquad\text{for every }a\text{ with }(s,a)\in\mathcal B_\pi.
\end{equation}
This includes a state completed at the endpoint $\tau\wedge\zeta_r$. Each request has conditional probability at least $p_0$ given the current history and the selection of its unfinished state.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A favorable update only raises the reference estimate or lowers a competing estimate. It keeps the reference action greedy, so \eqref{eq:greedy-pol-tie-breaking} retains that action. At a state not updated, the policy is unchanged. Induction therefore retains the reference action at every unfinished state, including during waits and after its last favorable update.

Every processed gap either reaches $\delta$ or receives $N$ increases of more than $\delta\alpha_k$. Later favorable updates at the same state cannot lower it. For each seeding step $k<T_s$, condition~\eqref{eq:comparison} gives $\alpha_j\le c_\alpha\alpha_k$ for every $j\ge T_s$, hence $\bar\alpha_{T_s}\le c_\alpha\alpha_k$. Summing these $N$ increases proves \eqref{eq:seedmargin}.
At most $|\mathcal S\times\mathcal A|$ pairs are processed, and the probability of each request is bounded below by \eqref{eq:localprob}.
\end{proof}
